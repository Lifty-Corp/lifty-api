# Campaigns

A Campaign is one channel of a Journey. It owns its start rules, its ordered steps (and so its count), templates or generated-writing instructions, the delays between its steps, its sending schedule and timezone, and the canonical sender_ids it may use. Business owns profile/voice revisions; Identity owns persons and accounts; Searches owns lead facts. No vendor, engine, rate-control, language or country field belongs here.

Start rules are alternatives; the first one due starts the Campaign once. `journey_start` starts it when the lead starts the Journey and stands alone. `sent` waits the saved delay after the confirmed send of a step of another Campaign in the same Journey. `unaccepted` waits business days after an unaccepted LinkedIn invitation. An Email Campaign may also use `{type:"linkedin_first_dm_skipped",campaign_ref:"<LinkedIn Campaign>"}` to start when that Campaign's first DM is skipped in review. The source Campaign belongs to the same Journey; this condition has no delay and never activates an inactive Email Campaign. A LinkedIn first delay counts from acceptance; the first email has none. Later delays count from this Campaign's previous confirmed send, in days or business days (Monday to Friday, Campaign timezone); some email providers allow only days. A business-day step shows `delay_minutes: 0` in previews and tests; its wait is applied at send time. Account limits or the sending window may delay a send but never shorten or expire pending work.

Templates mode saves one template per step, variants, or an approved catalog. Content lanes route by the lead's title: the first rule whose terms it contains, otherwise the default lane, including a missing title. The first email may have `cold` and `linkedin_bridge` openers: bridge applies when this Journey already confirmed a LinkedIn message, otherwise cold. The lane and opener are chosen once, when the Campaign first prepares for that lead, and hold for every step. Plain variants need exactly one option for each lane and opener. A catalog gives every template a unique `id` and its approved `arms` (`direct`, `pain`, `personal`, `strategic`); preserve authored `variant`, `coverage`, `fit` and `slots_spec` metadata. Optional `display_name` labels a template or variant (nonblank single-line text, at most 100 characters); renaming never changes its stable ID or route. A label alone does not turn a simple template into an authored catalog. A template's subject and text define its slots: Lifty fills each {placeholder} they use and nothing else. `slots_spec` is free-form guidance the writer reads about those slots; it never adds or removes one. Keep it exactly as saved. Saved research and Campaign instructions select the actual catalog IDs and one arm; this is not random A/B allocation. Each arm must have compatible templates throughout the sequence, and each email family must cover both first-email openers with the same subject. A first-email catalog record whose variant ends in `-both` preserves prior-email copy but is excluded from new first-contact selection. Fixed text is sent as saved; only the {slots} in the text are filled. Later emails reply in the pinned thread with its first email's subject. The saved steps determine the sequence count. Optional step `writing_instructions` supplies guidance for that message in generate mode (nonblank text, at most 10,000 characters; newlines and tabs are allowed). It complements campaign-wide instructions and Business voice. Omit absent names and step instructions rather than inserting defaults into historical revisions.

Read before editing. Draft edits require expected_version and the exact source revision_ref, change only the supplied fields and append an immutable unapproved revision. Editing never pauses, activates or releases holds. Publish approves one exact revision/digest and records actor/time only. Activate uses an approved revision for new Journey starts and sets intent; use the same action after a pause, there is no resume. Started Journeys keep their revisions, content, person, signature, account/thread and timing. A Campaign that starts after another Campaign joins new Journeys once that Campaign is activated too. An unchanged Campaign keeps its approval when a sibling changes. CRM customer exclusions require a separate explicit source choice; connection alone gives no permission to read customers. Unselected, file-only and neither allow activation and new Journey starts, and every saved exclusion and do-not-contact rule still applies. With neither, explain that unknown existing customers are not protected. Activation changes are refused with OUTREACH_CUSTOMER_LIST_NOT_CURRENT only while an enabled, connected required CRM source is missing or stale: read customer-exclusions status, explain its `crm_refresh` reason, and repair that source. Credentials or permission failures do not silently disable it. The founder can explicitly choose file-only or neither through customer-exclusions source-choice instead; uploading a file alone does not change that choice. Only enabled, authorized sources queue a durable refresh on connection or reconnection, even when the saved list is fresh; a queued refresh is pending, not current. Periodic recovery also retries failed sources. While required sources are not current no new Journeys start, even for active Campaigns; started Journeys continue on the last saved list. Re-activating an unchanged, operating Campaign remains a no-op. Structurally unavailable sources are report-only. A disconnected CRM is not held: outreach continues on the last saved list and alerts. A successful activation's `customer_list` records that decision.

Pause stops every automatic step of this Campaign in every started Journey, including follow-ups. Pending work is kept and begun or unknown sends reconcile. Other Campaigns continue where their start rules permit. A paused Campaign that starts the Journey starts no new Journeys. Connecting an account, readiness or approval never activates intent. Intent is not proof of a send or of account readiness.

One lead keeps the same named person across LinkedIn and email; several persons may be permitted. Account limits, edits, approval or account loss never move a lead to another person. The full sender signature is added before preview and approval and kept in approved bytes.

Test a revision with tests_post: exact revision_ref/digest/expected_version, an idempotent request_ref and up to 20 saved researched lead_refs. Preview uses the existing person allocation without assigning the lead. To compare a candidate, pass baseline_test_ref of a completed test: the same leads, the baseline's saved outputs as the before, and only the after composed from the leads' current saved research. Each sample lists `changes` (research, lead facts, person, Business revisions, composer prompts). The first email previews `cold` unless `opener` asks for `linkedin_bridge`. tests_get discovers receipts after a lost response; test_detail reads outputs. A failed sample carries `failure`: tell the founder its `message`, fix the named step if any, and preview again; do not guess another cause. A queued or running test is not output, approval or sending permission. Tested leads are examples, never the recipient list: activation starts every eligible lead in the Journey's audience. Confirm whether feedback belongs to Business voice or this Campaign's templates/instructions before saving it.

`reviews_get` reads the workspace's saved review queue; filter by channel or review_status and use next_after to continue. `message_get` reads the exact message, person, account, saved conversation with that person across their accounts and immutable revisions. `history_complete:false` means the conversation is unknown or truncated; an empty history then proves nothing. An email step whose `subject` is null replies in the first email's thread. A history message with `copy_source: manual` is one the person typed directly in LinkedIn: Lifty recorded it but did not write or send it. Check the recipient, named person, account and full saved bytes with the reviewer before a decision. Sent requires a confirmed receipt; planned or scheduled messages have not been delivered.

For an actual still-unapproved per-lead draft, `message_revisions_post` saves a corrected new pending revision with the exact source_digest and review state. The old revision, person/signature and template/prompt/selection provenance are kept; reusable policy and other leads are unchanged. It never approves or sends; approved, sent or superseded messages cannot be corrected here. Read the new revision before asking for approval.

`message_review_post` records the reviewer's explicit approve, skip or retry for a LinkedIn or email message, with source_digest and expected_review_status from that exact read. Approve releases only those bytes for the already-active Campaign, under its current account, schedule and send checks. It does not activate or promise a send. Skip closes only this channel's branch; skipping an already-connected first LinkedIn DM allows its already-active Email Campaign to start. It never suppresses the person globally. Give a reason when skipping. A not-sent message provides a bounded `not_sent_cause`; do not infer delivery certainty from that cause. Retry re-checks the account and sender and atomically approves that failed revision. A disconnected account leaves the failure unchanged. A begun, uncertain or confirmed attempt cannot be retried. Members and LIFT operators use the same review. A stale or uncertain decision needs a fresh read; never retry a provider send yourself. A message with `action_blocked_reason: historical_engine_retired` is retained history: read it, but do not edit, approve, retry, skip or rewrite it. A missing sender revision alone is a separate provenance error and does not prove retirement. Optional decision `feedback` records the explicit copy rating (`up` or `down`) and note atomically with approve or retry; approving an edited draft does not imply an up rating.

`review_settings_get` reads a separate choice for LinkedIn and email: `approve_each`, or `send_and_sample` with a 10%, 20% or 50% sample, or 0% to send without a sample. Save only the chosen channels through `review_settings_patch` with expected_version. The choice applies to new drafts; it never releases pending work. Flagged messages, replies and requested follow-ups always wait for a person. `samples_get` returns confirmed sent samples to rate. Use `message_rating_post` with the exact source_digest and up/down feedback. Confirm whether a note belongs only to this message, the Campaign, or Business voice. Saving feedback and changing a Campaign draft or commercial voice are separate operations with separate receipts and version guards; never claim a reusable writing change from a rating alone.

`replies_get` reads waiting reply turns and due requested follow-ups. Read a focused turn with `reply_get` before deciding. Render its required `view.label`, `view.detail` and `view.actions` as supplied: SQL owns their meaning; do not derive status from legacy fields. `reply_review_post` requires a new UUID `decision_id` for each user intent and the exact `view.version` as `expected_version`. Retain that same ID, version, action and text across retries of an identical intent after a lost response. Do not use source_digest as a reply decision selector. `send` approves the saved draft, or edits when `text` is supplied (trimmed, 1–20,000 characters). `skip` suppresses this turn only. `recover` and `retry` use this same command only when the view offers them; neither is permission to send directly. A `stale` outcome is normal: display the returned current view, dispatch nothing and let the founder decide again. `accepted` and `replayed` also return the current view; a dispatch ID identifies the saved approval for existing ingress, not confirmed delivery. Sending uses the original receiving account and conversation or mailbox thread. Queued, processing and uncertain do not mean sent. Poll boundedly for the returned view's delivery evidence; retain pending state if the wait ends. A missing verified account or conversation is not permission to switch accounts. Slack's editor alone has a 3,000-character limit; API edits allow 20,000.

`message_rewrite_post` requests a new pending revision with the exact source_digest, pending review state and an idempotent request_ref. It keeps earlier bytes and never approves. Read the source message's `rewrite_work` with bounded polling. When done, read its exact replacement_message_ref and review the new copy before approval. A failed or uncertain operation is not a new draft; never repeat it blindly. `lead_stop_post` is the separate explicit stop-all choice: confirm:true plus request_ref creates the lead's manual_stop barrier across every LinkedIn and email Campaign and requests provider cancellation. `provider_stops_pending` means cancellation still needs reconciliation. Their inbound replies remain available; stopping never sends a reply or unsubscribes the conversation.

The runtime read groups started Journeys by the executable version and revision they keep, with recorded gate reasons only. Read back after uncertain writes; never repeat creation, approval or activation blindly. Unknown state is unavailable, not inactive, empty, failed or healthy. references.writing and references.anti_slop guide draft copy; references.common owns transport.

Campaign reads and saves return `writing_recommendations`: phrases from references.anti_slop found in the draft's saved templates, by step, template and field. They never block saving, testing, approval or activation. Show them to the founder with a suggested rewrite. If the founder keeps the wording, keep it and do not raise it again. Absent means the list could not be read, not that the copy is clean.

Campaign reads and saves also return `slots_spec_warnings` when a template's `slots_spec` names a {placeholder} its own subject and text do not use, by step and template (publish reports the revision it approved). The writer follows the spec, so an unused name misleads it. Ask the founder whether the slot belongs in the text, then fix the text or the spec. A spec may name a placeholder on purpose, such as "No {main_focus}"; then keep it and do not raise it again. Warnings never block saving, testing, approval or activation, and absent means there are none.
